Saputra, G.A.M. (2019) 'Kajian Instrumentasi dan Organologi Gendang Beleq Sanggar Mertaq Mi Lombok Tengah Nusa Tenggara Barat', Sorai: Jurnal Pengkajian dan Penciptaan Musik, 12(2), pp. 57-70. doi:10.33153/sorai.v12i2.2837.

Read in full. Field study of one group (Mertaq Mi, Setanggor, Central Lombok), and the author warns it may not represent every group.

- Name: gendang is drum; beleq means big. The ensemble is named for its unusually large drums.
- Drums: gendang mame (male, higher "dag") and gendang nine (female, lower "dug"), also lanang and wadon. Struck with a mallet (pemantok) and slapped with the palm. Interlocking playing.
- Drum materials: usually tap wood, also randu, boroq, nitaq. This group uses randu and goat skin. Its drums are 94 cm long.
- Melodic instruments: suling and reyong (bronze here). Rhythmic: gendang, gong beleq, kempur, petuq, rincik, kenceng.
- Oncer is another name for petuq, which controls the tempo. Gong closes phrases. Kempur fills the count of phrases.
- Kenceng resembles ceng-ceng. Rincik fills gaps when kenceng is not playing.
- Iron gongs are cheaper and lighter for processions, and bronze sounds better.
- History: people believe it dates from the kingdoms, but when and in which kingdom is unknown and has no scientific proof. A group leader relates a community story of it accompanying royal soldiers training or going to war.
- Use: emphasises processions (arak-arakan): weddings, official functions, cultural ceremonies.
- Decline: supporters report falling interest. Kecimol is now often chosen for nyongkolan.
